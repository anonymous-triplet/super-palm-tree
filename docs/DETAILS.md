# City Ground Zero — Live City + SUMO Traffic

A live Godot 4 city bundled with the official Godot 4.7.2 Linux x86_64 runtime, Eclipse SUMO 1.25.0, its TraCI tools and shared libraries, Python 3.14, and Godot's pre-generated import cache. No system-wide Godot, SUMO, Python, or first-launch asset import is required.

## Run it

- Run `./run_control_room.sh` from the repository root. It sets up what is missing (asking first), then starts SUMO, the citizen planner, the city, the partner gateway and the Brain and Guardian stand-ins, and stops them all when the city closes.
- Run `./run_control_room.sh` for a single native Godot control-room application: left city summary, direct central city viewport, right controls/live things, and the bottom run log (the live lines of the run logs: what the twin detected and did, and every message between the twin, the City Brain and the Guardian). It does not open a second app, overlay the scene, or transfer camera images. Use the complete 4-column, 16-camera button grid to switch the central live Godot viewport instantly, or use `Random Citizen` and `Random Car` to frame and follow a live person or vehicle using the same third-person behavior as object selection.

The panel's traffic controls are pause and resume. The 49 signals run their own cycle under SUMO; their phases are not shown or exposed.

The complete right-hand control panel uses one consistent responsive content system: Operations, Asset Map, Utilities, and Citizen Society all receive the same 10 px horizontal padding, section spacing, scrollbar allowance, and draggable-width behavior. Both sidebars and their contents open together at their true minimum draggable widths—220 px on the left and 380 px on the right—so the simulation receives the available space by default. Their internal layouts remain intact—four camera columns, paired follow/highlight controls, a readable single-column asset legend, two-column utility cut controls, full-width meters, and full-width citizen cards. The redundant Live SUMO Things list has been removed; authoritative traffic totals and status remain in the left metrics and event log. The **Citizen Society** tab presents explicit **WHO / WHAT / WHY** facts: stable citizen and household identity, job and personality; action, status, target or interaction partner; then the current objective and its causal reason. Age, employer, daily agenda, work order, trip, society clock, route, transport feedback and decision authority remain available in collapsed diagnostic sections.

## Web dashboard (an MCP client)

`./run_control_room.sh --dashboard` starts `tools/dashboard/dashboard_server.py` next to the city. The server holds no simulation data: it serves the page (`tools/dashboard/web`) and relays MCP between the browser and the MCP server using MCP's HTTP + Server-Sent-Events transport (`GET /mcp/sse`, `POST /mcp/message`). The page is itself an MCP client: it initialises, lists the tools and opens one `read_city` stream. That single whole-city snapshot carries status, all device readings, vehicles, citizens, incidents, emergency units, parking, earthquakes, events and panel state every 100 ms, so the dashboard does not multiply the load with one subscription per card. The dashboard is also a complete operator surface: its controls invoke the same MCP tools as external clients for time, weather, simulation speed, signals, incidents, earthquakes, replay, cameras, following, physical-device focus, asset and utility-network highlights, the full-window viewport layout, sectors, parking, waste, utilities and street lights. The Payload page can inspect the shared feed, the latest command response, or any streamable MCP tool as a live in-place JSON tree or raw text.

## Sectors, smart car parks and earthquakes

- **Sectors A to P.** The 8 x 8 block city is cut into a 4 x 4 grid of sectors of 2 x 2 blocks (`scripts/city_sectors.gd`), tinted and lettered on the ground (`scripts/sector_overlay.gd`; Assets tab: "City sectors A to P"). Every device, building, car park and earthquake knows its sector.
- **Smart car parks** (`sumo/parking_lots.json`, sited by `tools/compute_parking_lots.gd`; eight 12-bay lots in different sectors). Cars choose a lot, stop at the kerb in front of its barrier and the lot checks its own occupancy (`scripts/parking_lots.py`, run by the SUMO bridge). With room: the barrier lifts, the car is handed to Godot, drives in, parks, stays a while, drives out and is put back into SUMO with a route out of the city. When the lot is full (or its barrier is set to closed): the barrier stays shut, a red warning triangle flashes over it, and after a short wait the car drives on to the nearest lot that still has room (or back into traffic). The board over each barrier shows the count (`05/12`, `FULL`, `CLOSED`). The presenter is `scripts/parking/parking_lot_presenter.gd`; cars never pop (the same hand-off used by the emergency fleet). Operators can set a lot's barrier to `auto`, `open` or `closed` over MCP (`set_parking_barrier`).
- **Seismic sensors and earthquakes.** One strong-motion accelerograph per sector (`SEIS-A.4` ...; metrics ground acceleration, ground velocity and shaking intensity; the read result also carries the last two seconds of the three-axis waveform at 20 Hz). `scripts/earthquake_system.gd` shakes one sector or the whole city with a magnitude: ground motion attenuates with distance from the sector, the camera shakes, and a share of the buildings in the area collapses with a staggered, animated pancake collapse (`scripts/building_collapse.gd`, slabs clipped by `shaders/building_slab.gdshader`, dust and debris particles, a rubble pile). The Incidents tab has the controls (sector, magnitude, trigger, clean up); `clear_earthquakes` removes every trace and restores every building.

## Sensors and actuators over MCP (live)

`./run_control_room.sh` opens the city window and then turns the terminal into an **MCP terminal** (`city-mcp>`). It is a
real MCP client: it starts `tools/mcp/city_mcp_server.py`, performs the `initialize` / `notifications/initialized`
handshake, lists the tools and sends every line you type as a JSON-RPC `tools/call` over stdio. The server forwards the
call to the running Godot city (`scripts/sim_api.gd`, a local socket on `CITY_SIM_API_PORT`), which answers from the live
scene at that instant and applies commands through the same code the control-room buttons use. Closing the terminal
(`exit`) closes the city; `CITY_NO_CLI=1 ./run_control_room.sh` keeps the old window-only behaviour.

```
city-mcp> ↑ ↓                                         # browse the commands; or type: the matching function appears faintly, ◀ ▶ other matching functions, Tab pastes it
city-mcp> examples                                 # numbered ready-made calls   (example 3 runs the third)
city-mcp> read_sensor(id="CAN-F.1-1")             # a SUBSCRIPTION: a live line every 100 ms until q / Ctrl+C
city-mcp> read_sensor(id="CAN-F.1-1", once=True)   # one reading;  interval_s=0.5 sets the rate, duration_s=20 stops after 20 s
city-mcp> watch_sensor id=CAN-F.1-1 metric=bin_weight duration_s=20     # timed capture with a trend line
city-mcp> waste_bin_report min_fill_percent=40
city-mcp> list_actuators family=power_switch
city-mcp> set_power_switch id=SWT-F.3 state=open    # real change: the feeder goes dark, readings show before -> after
city-mcp> set_street_light id=LGT-A.1-1 mode=dim level=0.4
city-mcp> set_weather weather=storm
city-mcp> protocol on                              # print the raw JSON-RPC traffic
city-mcp> raw {"jsonrpc":"2.0","id":9,"method":"tools/call","params":{"name":"city_status","arguments":{}}}
```

The terminal clears the screen and shows a header: what the terminal is, every tool in one line, live ids to try, and
a few examples. **The data functions stream by themselves.** `read_sensor`, `read_sensors`, `city_status`, `sensor_summary`
and `waste_bin_report` are live MCP tools: when the client asks for progress (a `progressToken`, which the terminal sends
whenever a person is at the keyboard) they send a live update every `interval_s` as `notifications/progress` until the call is
cancelled with the standard `notifications/cancelled` (`q` or Ctrl+C in the terminal) or `duration_s` ends, and the final
result holds every sample. `once=True` returns one reading. A client that does not ask for progress gets one reading
unless it passes `duration_s`, in which case the call returns the whole series. The terminal adds nothing to the call: it
sends exactly what was typed and prints what the function sends back, so any MCP client behaves the same way.

Tools: `city_status`, `list_sensors`, `read_sensor`, `read_sensors`, `watch_sensor`, `sensor_summary`, `waste_bin_report`,
`list_actuators`, `list_commands`, `command_actuator`, `set_weather`, `set_time_of_day`, `set_simulation_speed`,
`pause_simulation`, `resume_simulation`, `set_power_switch`, `set_valve`, `set_street_light`,
`set_sector_service`, `empty_waste_bin` (plus MCP resources `city://status`, `city://sensor/{id}`). Any other MCP client
can use the same server: `CITY_SIM_API_PORT=<port> python3 tools/mcp/city_mcp_server.py` (the port is printed in the event
log, and `run_control_room.sh` exports it).

Device ids say where a device is: type-sector.block, such as `AIR-D.3`, with a number when a block holds several of a type, such as `LGT-A.1-3` (blocks are 1 2 / 3 4 inside a sector). Types: `TRAF-`, `CAM-`, `AIR-`, `NOISE-`, `WX-`, `FLOOD-`, `UTIL-` (environment), `PWR-`/`WTR-` (service meters),
`CAN-` street-bin and `BIN-` refuse-container **weight sensors**, `VLV-` water valves, `SWT-`
power switches, `LGT-` smart lights, `IRR-` irrigation, `RSV-` reservoirs, `GRID-01` (city-wide), `SEC-` sector services (`SEC-A`), `LOT-` car parks, `FAC-` factories, `SEIS-` seismic sensors. The Sensors tab
lists every one of these families (19 categories).

**Bin weight sensors.** Every street waste can (96) and every green container (44) carries a load cell. Citizens who buy a
drink walk to the nearest bin with room and throw the can or cup in (hand release, arc, drop into the opening); people
also bring a bag when they spend free time, and the sanitation worker's bin job deposits bags. The weight and fill level
rise on the next sample; when the refuse truck tips a container over its hopper, the container reads empty.

Tests: `tests/test_sim_api.gd` (every family, live reads, every actuator class, citizens raising a bin's weight, socket
round trip) and `tests/mcp/` (`python3 -m unittest discover -s twin/tests/mcp`: protocol conformance against a fake city,
terminal session in a pseudo-terminal, and an end-to-end run against the headless city).

## Partner layers: City Brain and Guardian

The twin talks to the other two layers of the triplet through its partner gateway (`twin/src/tools/mcp/twin_gateway.py`). The gateway serves the twin's MCP tools over Streamable HTTP inside mutual TLS 1.3, at `https://127.0.0.1:8740/mcp` by default. Everything about the links is configured in `twin/twin.conf`: the endpoint, the PEM key pairs, the trusted authorities, each partner's address and identity, and what each partner may call.

For every incident, the twin reports the emergency calls to the City Brain (`report_incident`). The Brain has the Guardian verify the report. The Guardian reads the twin's own data to do so (`read_vehicle`, `read_sector`). The Brain then answers each call through the twin's actuator tool `dispatch_units`, and the caller phones the chosen unit's facility. The Brain's authority over dispatch is a lease that the gateway renews every 2 s. If the Brain is unreachable, or a call goes 10 s without an answer, the twin's own dispatcher takes over with the same rule (the closest free unit of the kind asked for). `twin/tests/layers` checks on 600 random fleets that the stand-in Brain picks exactly the unit the twin would.

The stand-ins in `twin/mocks/` are separate processes with their own keys and `standins.conf`. Every exchange appears with a time stamp in three places: the incident's run log (`logs/`), the control room's bottom window and Timeline tab, and the dashboard's Timeline page. The Interface Card is [twin/INTERFACE.md](../twin/INTERFACE.md).

## Time, weather, speed and sensor history

The unified control room has one shared 24-hour city clock overlaid in the simulation canvas's top-right corner. Its compact state shows the current time, day, period and sun/moon position; the first click only expands it, and time selection is armed after that opening gesture is released. Its close control returns it to the compact state. Dawn, Noon, Dusk and Night shortcuts remain in the control panel. Sun angle, physical sky, ambient light, fog, exposure, 256 adaptive roadway/sidewalk luminaires, exact imported building-window surfaces and vehicle headlamps follow the clock. All 64 districts receive the same four block-face street fixtures. Each enlarged street light is a complete roadway luminaire with an integrated high-output emissive LED lens and a matching downward beam; its visible model and lamp remain available at overview distance without extending shadow draw distance. Building windows are the model's own polygons, found per model by `scripts/building_window_builder.gd`: separate glazing surfaces where the model has them, the pane quads of atlas-textured models (selected triangle by triangle from the atlas colour and shape), and a UV patch map for the one tower whose windows exist only as painted pixels. Every window has its own state (level, tint, flicker) in a per-building texture, so any subset can be lit; `scripts/building_window_controller.gd` schedules them by time of day (a few in the small hours, most in the evening, each at its own threshold) and exposes `set_window_level`, `set_window_tint`, `set_building_fraction`, `set_building_powered`, `set_global_fraction`, `get_windows` and `find_windows_near` for later features. The lit windows nearest the camera also cast a real, soft warm light from a pooled set of lights. Entrance lanterns are placed on the street-facing facade (toward the nearest road) by scanning for a flat stretch of solid wall at 2.4–3.4 m that is clear of windows, doors, glazing and edges; a building with no such wall gets none rather than a misplaced one. The city clock runs on simulated time: `WorldTime` sets how many clock seconds pass per simulated second (real time by default, with fast presets), and the simulation speed buttons are delivered by SUMO itself so the clock, weather and citizens stay in step. Imported whole-wall emission is disabled, preventing roofs, plaster and garage doors from glowing. Citizen identity, agendas, travel, employment, physical work and social interactions remain authoritative in the existing Society Kernel and NPC controller—the environment layer never hides or freezes them. If a refuse round was not called during the day, the sanitation truck automatically starts its collection route after 20:00.

Weather changes automatically every six simulated hours or can be set to Clear, Cloudy, Rain, Fog or Storm. Conditions change precipitation, visibility, illumination, pedestrian pace, safe SUMO traffic speed and the readings reported by environmental sensors. The 1×, 2×, 4× and 8× controls accelerate Godot physics, animations, schedules and timers together with every intervening SUMO traffic and incident-engine step; accelerated modes do not skip simulation logic.

The **Sensors** tab covers all 88 physical map-linked streams: traffic detectors, street cameras, air-quality monitors, noise monitors, weather stations, flood sensors, utility gateways, and every sector power/water meter. It is organised in three steps: choose a category chip (All, Traffic, Air, Noise, Weather, Flood, Utilities — each with its live device count), optionally filter by ID or reading, then pick a device from a compact one-row-per-device list (eight per page, each with its current primary reading); a *Show this device in the city* button frames its model. Pressing a device frames its actual city model; pressing a reading opens its history immediately below. The same persistent outline/tag interaction now applies when clicking operational power meters, water meters, technician meter cabinets, poles, cables, sources, reservoirs, reservoir meters, waste containers, response stations, sensors and actuators directly in the world. Buildings, ground, people and vehicles retain their earlier exclusions. Traffic values come from vehicles inside the device's physical range; air values respond to nearby exhaust severity and stopped engines; noise responds to nearby traffic and people; flood depth accumulates and drains with live weather; utility gateways and meters respond immediately to reserve, demand, flow, and actual service cuts. Sampling continues by wall time even while simulation time is paused. Graph history is ordered by sample sequence—so pausing, rewinding, or jumping the city clock cannot draw lines outside the plot—and retains the latest 240 samples for that simulation instance.

Registered city devices and meters can also be focused directly in the viewport. Their focused state uses the same persistent outline/tag path as sensor selection. Camera orbit, pan, pivot and drag preserve focus; a plain click elsewhere or selecting another object releases it. Citizens, vehicles, responders, buildings, backdrop scenery and the floor are intentionally excluded from this visual treatment.

Camera controls: click a vehicle or person to enter a persistent framed-follow view. Third-person follow is held in world space, and CityLife always updates the selected car before the camera reads its transform in that same frame. Press `V` to enter first-person; the pointer is captured and ordinary mouse movement looks around immediately, with unrestricted 360-degree yaw and a safe vertical pitch bound. The camera origin is placed beyond the selected model's projected world bounds, so looking backward never puts the lens inside a citizen or vehicle. Press `V` again to return to the same exterior frame.

The city camera keeps its original calm movement with map-viewer controls: left-drag pans along the ground, right-drag orbits around the point under the cursor, and the wheel zooms in fixed steps toward the point under the cursor. A click without dragging still frames a person or vehicle. `Alt`/`Ctrl` + right-drag is free-look; Blender-style MMB orbit, Shift+MMB pan and Ctrl+MMB dolly remain available; `W/A/S/D` or the arrow keys plus `Q/E` move the camera, which stays inside the city area. `Esc` detaches from a selected person/vehicle; when a control-room street feed is active, the same key releases that feed, zooms out to an unobstructed three-quarter exterior view of its physical roadside camera, and restores free-camera control. Press `R`, `F`, or `H` for the standard camera locations.

The preserved traffic layer keeps a followed SUMO car alive by issuing a legal cross-city `changeTarget` reroute three edges before its perimeter exit. In the rare case an external removal still occurs, the rendered car coasts visibly for 2.2 seconds and the camera detaches before the pooled model is hidden; the view can no longer remain attached to nothing at the edge of the map.

## Incidents and emergency response

The control room's **Incidents** tab triggers nine incident types played out by SUMO with real traffic: rear-end collision, red-light (T-bone) collision, unsafe overtake, breakdown, vehicle fire, lost load, building fire, water main break and signal failure. **Use selected car** pins a crash to the car you have selected; **Pick building** starts a fire in the building you click. A trigger always produces its incident: if a pinned car or junction never offers a safe geometry, the nearest feasible one is used and the timeline says so.

Each incident card follows the scene live (phase, units, timeline) and offers **Watch** (chase the striking car, then frame the scene from an unobstructed viewpoint, a burning façade from across the street), **Replay** (the frozen collision from orbit, chase, street, overhead and driver cameras), **Resolve** (fast-forwards the same legal clearing path, never deleting anything in view) and extra units. The fleet board shows every unit's state.

Units are real vehicles: they board at their stations, pull out along validated curves, drive in response mode (red lights crossed only when the junction is clear), stop as one block behind the scene, and their crews walk only where no vehicle can drive. Police flag a stop/go lane closure and guide queued cars past one at a time through the held oncoming lane; at a blocked junction they run the signals themselves. Tows load wrecks onto the flatbed and carry them to the tow yard; ambulances transport patients; units drive away only once their whole crew is aboard. When the local fleet cannot help (all busy, or trapped behind the scene), mutual-aid units enter from the city edge. `scripts/test_incidents.py` runs all nine types in one simulation and fails on any unresolved incident, unscripted collision or leftover road control. Design: [docs/INCIDENT_SYSTEM_ARCHITECTURE.md](docs/INCIDENT_SYSTEM_ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Fix 78 active meter work, grounded bends and finger-edge grips

Fix 78 retains Fix 77's society, deconflicted movement and spatial-hash performance repairs, then makes active physical work the presentation priority. At least ten citizens must currently be stationary and performing a physical clip on a matching prop; at least five must operate water or power meters, with two of each kind. Poles, walking, conversation, recording-only work, pointing and passive proximity do not count. The standing forward-inspection pose now keeps both legs and feet planted, while held assets use per-model side-edge, upper-edge, top or handle anchors at the articulated fingers rather than the wrist or middle of the palm.

A deterministic `SocietyKernel` preserves 200 citizens in 80 households, employs them through 13 institutions, maintains a multiplex social graph, and advances individual health, fatigue, funds, household membership, employment, age and daily agendas even when a citizen is not rendered. Thirty-two stable citizen records are bound to the detailed models in the street; rendering distance does not create or destroy identity.

Daily behavior is selected from time-based agendas and world state. Institutions own budgets, inventory, employees, service backlogs, and physical assets. A citizen begins an explicit work order, travels on the legal sidewalk graph, performs a compatible physical action at the assigned asset, and commits the result on completion: the order closes, asset condition changes, institutional backlog/budget changes, and wages are recorded. Ten presentation workers are selected from the real backlog for visually legible physical props and turn over to subsequent assignments instead of remaining permanent set dressing. The final repeated sample sustained 13–16 valid physical-prop workers.

Social behavior is event-based rather than an endless paired loop. Six role-compatible opening events reserve two grounded slots and a shared prop, then run exactly three complementary motion-capture phases. Each phase identifies one speaker, one listener and one tangible item custodian. Parcel, goods and document custody changes are synchronized with the visible hand item and stored in the authoritative event history; the final item persists in the recipient's inventory. Completion updates the relationship, releases the prop/reservations and sends both citizens to their next agenda/work assignments on foot.

Every embodied trip has an origin, destination, travel mode, expected duration, progress, and arrival state. The kernel samples live aggregate SUMO status when the bridge is available and records a local fallback explicitly when it is not; SUMO still remains authoritative for vehicle motion. This does not pretend that every off-screen citizen owns a rendered car. State can be snapshotted and restored, and the same seed produces the same population, households, agendas, institutions, and relationships. ## Real-scale props, planted citizens and prop-specific interactions

**Props.** Everything citizens and sensors interact with is built at its real-world size and is never
rescaled to "look right". Scanned PBR models come from Poly Haven (CC0, `assets/props/polyhaven/`); things no
free scan exists for (post box, dumpster, ATM, bus stop, bicycle on a rack, cone, vending machine, parking
meter, drinking fountain, payphone with a lifting handset, market counter with a cash drawer, park bench) are
built procedurally to their real dimensions (`scripts/props/`). `PropCatalog` lists each kind (height,
front, hinged parts, contact anchors), `PropFactory` picks the right variant out of a glTF "variant sheet",
sets the lowest visible point on the ground, re-pivots lids/caps/doors onto their hinges and creates named
anchors, then bakes static meshes per material. `StreetFurniturePlanner` lays out ~600 pieces by rule along
each block's kerb: benches in pairs with a bin at the end, planters flanking seating, hydrants before corners,
cabinets, meters, bike racks, fountains, vending machines and payphones, each facing the footpath, aligned to
the edge and rejected if it touches a building, a road, a street light or another piece.

**Citizens and interactions.** Every activity is a real motion-capture performance from the Microsoft Rocketbox
animation library (MIT, same `Bip01` skeleton as the avatars): sit down on a bench, a dozen seated idles, stand up;
crouch in / idle / gesture / out; work at waist and chest height; phone calls and texting; drinking; reading
documents; a newspaper; waiting, stretching, looking around; hand-truck holding. 270 clips (135 per gender) are
baked by `tools/bake_rocketbox_clips.gd` into `assets/rocketbox/clips_{m,f}.res` (the capture's own root motion and
turn are extracted so a sit-down really steps the body back onto the seat) and measured by
`tools/measure_rocketbox_clips.gd` (`clip_stats.json`: where the hands, head and pelvis were). Nothing is posed by
code. `ActivityLibrary` picks the posture class that matches each prop's working height and places the person so the
actor's hands land on the prop (`stand_transform`); `ClipDirector` runs the clips, applies the root motion and puts
carried objects in the hand. A small skeleton modifier (`citizen_ik.gd`) only keeps the lowest foot contact on the
pavement. Source FBX files are not stored in the repo (they are fetched from the Rocketbox repository by the bake tool).

**Society.** Twenty roles (eight new: street vendor, shopkeeper, security guard, paramedic, electrician,
freight handler, office clerk, amenities attendant) with their own props. Every visible citizen has needs
(energy, hunger, thirst, social, fun) that fall with simulated time; when one gets pressing between tasks they
walk to a fitting public amenity (fountain, vending machine, coffee cart, bench, payphone), use it, pay if
needed and go back to work. People who meet on the footpath sometimes stop and talk, which strengthens their
relationship in the society kernel.

Tests: `tests/test_prop_and_rig_quality.gd` (real sizes, grounding, every played clip exists for both genders, nobody
stands inside a prop), `test_mocap_activities.gd` (live city: hands on the prop, seated pelvis on the seat, facing),

## Citizen embodiment, work and interaction

The active population is 32 completely replaced, realistically proportioned Microsoft Rocketbox citizens using 19 distinct higher-definition adult models. Twelve wardrobe-aware roles—courier, sanitation worker, utilities technician, parks worker, clinic assistant, transit steward, market stocker, resident, road-maintenance technician, building-maintenance technician, public-information technician, and horticulture specialist—run 24 concrete station jobs. Dedicated delivery, construction, gardener, medical, business, chef, and civilian wardrobe families make the assigned profession readable in the scene. The jobs retain distinct external props including a manhole, safety cone, exterior HVAC unit, fire exit, billboard, community poster, greenhouse, and planted flower pot.

Six visible two-character events can open alongside those individual jobs: parcel handoff, clinic check-in, power-meter service briefing, market purchase, street-cleaning review, and transit route assistance. Each event reserves two separate naturally spaced 1.8 m slots, makes both characters face one another, assigns different participant responsibilities, and advances both actors through synchronized complementary animation phases. All 36 named phases map to deliberate Rocketbox motion-capture sources—presenting, receiving, speaking, listening, recording, inspecting, pointing, and acknowledging—rather than generated neutral-pose offsets. The live action text changes with the exact animation phase. Selecting either participant exposes the shared event, partner, role and causal purpose in the Citizen Society panel. The event then completes and releases both people; it is not an ambient infinite loop.

The city contains 64 exclusive physical activity sites plus six shared event props. Every station now has at least three distinct, supported work stages rather than a lone look/idle pose. Role sequences combine access/open, item pickup/collection, physical operation/application/repair, inspection/testing, result recording and placement/securement. Parcels, waste bags, medical supplies, market goods, mail, documents and notices are handled visibly; phones, cups and watering cans appear only during compatible stages. Long source-animation tails are bounded to 1.6–4.0 second work beats so a complete sequence is readable during play without changing animation speed or the skeleton.

A hard runtime invariant selects ten non-paired workers from actual institutional work orders. At least five operate dedicated water/power meter props, with at least two of each type; utility poles are explicitly excluded. Only a stationary `performing_*` state, a visible matching job prop, close station contact and an audited physical work clip count. Conversation, pointing, note-taking, person-to-person interaction, walking and walk-and-carry never count. The Fix 78 repeated acceptance run measured 13–16 simultaneous valid physical-prop workers. The stricter activity-quality sample observed 16 complete three-stage sequences, five held-item kinds, three tool kinds and zero inert stationary states. `Random Citizen` applies the same current-state distinction: all 48 tested selections were readable non-walking actions.

Citizens use a true 1.75–1.90 m human scale; every workstation and held tool is calibrated to that body size and grounded at the sidewalk. Physical job motion includes planted deep crouch, low inspection, placing a ground item, opening a ground container, mid-height repair, panel knock, latch pull, label inspection, surface pickup/repair, watering, planting, and harvesting. The rejected standing bend is reconstructed with its neutral lower chain while retaining authored head/arm motion, so both feet stay flat. Every phone, meter reader, paper, box, bag, cone, cup and watering can attaches through a finger cradle computed from the first articulated finger joints. Orientation-aware contact anchors put an actual edge, top or handle at the fingers; the final 81-object gate measured 12.2 cm minimum wrist-to-grip distance, 3.37 cm minimum solid-object wrist clearance and zero wrist penetration. The models and mocap share the authored Bip01 production rig. The installer admits only target-valid rotation tracks, keeps controlled pelvis weight shift, strips root motion, strips all scale keys, and strips non-pelvis translation keys; this prevents stretched limbs and lets the sidewalk planner retain position authority without runtime inverse kinematics. Rocketbox animation now evaluates first in the render frame, a root-only foot/toe lock runs second, and camera/control readers run third; this removes the former one-frame 0.45–0.57 m crouch hover without changing or stretching the skeleton. The final gate measured 0.0000 m error over 531 live crouch frames and 3,552 explicit transition samples. At startup 28 citizens are already active—including 12 in finite reciprocal events—and four are travelling, preventing the city from opening as one mass commute.

Movement uses the connected sidewalk graph, direction-relative right-hand lanes, a predictive 1.35 m citizen-separation floor, forward-only steering, short yielding and a deterministic lateral sidestep. Already-close citizens may take only a separating step, so safety does not become a deadlock. Route-entry targets are forward, staggered and checked against current occupancy; a blocked connector is replaced without teleporting. Power poles and traffic lights use their ground support footprint for pedestrian collision instead of the elevated arm's full AABB. Spatial hashes prevent movement cost from growing into a frame-time spiral when many people walk at once.

Untrusted imported spawns enter once at their nearest safe graph node; subsequent job changes visibly walk through a validated station connector instead of teleporting or drawing a diagonal route through a building. Job props are resolved against roads, rendered building bounds and every other visible prop. The same rendered-bounds pass grounds ambient props to the 0.16 m surface and relocates conflicts. ## Factories and air quality

Two factories stand on edge blocks at opposite corners of the 8 x 8 grid (`FAC-D.1` in sector D and `FAC-M.4` in sector M), each a compound of halls and stacks in the `factory_buildings` group. They work in shifts of 75–140 s with breaks of 12–30 s, staggered so they never pause together. While working, every stack feeds a dense column of short-lived puffs and releases large gas clouds that rise, spread to 40–60 m radius, drift with the reported wind plus a pull toward the city centre (the urban heat island), thin out and vanish after two to three minutes. Rain and storms wash them out faster. A collapsed factory stops emitting (`scripts/factory_emissions.gd`, `shaders/factory_smog.gdshader`).

Every sector now has an air monitor (`AIR-F.1` to `AIR-20`), reporting PM2.5, PM10, NO2, SO2, vehicle smoke and the air-quality index. Factory air follows the seismic rule: full strength anywhere in the factory's sector, then falling with distance from that sector, so the neighbouring sectors read a weaker version and far sectors almost nothing; passing clouds add a little on top. The MCP tools are `read_air_quality` (live, per sector), `set_factory` and `set_all_factories`; the panel has Shifts / Run / Stop under the environment controls and a Factories entry in the asset map; the dashboard has an **Air & factories** page and clouds on the city map. Checks: `tests/test_factories.gd`, `tests/render_factories.gd`.

## Smooth running

`twin/tests/profile_runtime.gd` measures the real control room against a live bridge (frame-time distribution, hitches, cost per script, what is drawn, and how much a moving vehicle's on-screen speed changes per frame). What keeps the frame steady:

- **Vehicle clock.** A packet's arrival is known only to the nearest frame, so small clock errors are eased out instead of being corrected each frame. Vehicles still follow exactly the SUMO positions.
- **No full scans in the frame.** Prop overlap questions use a grid and remembered bounds; parking paths are worked out once; the society advances in 0.1 s batches of the exact elapsed time; street, facade and window lights update five times a second (commands apply at once).
- **Sampling spread over frames.** A panel refresh updates the panels on one frame. It then takes the environment's sensors on the next frame and the other devices on the one after. The sensor history (about 2,000 series) is appended 150 devices per frame. While someone streams `read_city`, the sample is gathered ahead of the request over two frames. The 555 device rows of `read_city` and `read_all` are built on a worker thread and turned into JSON there. A device command discards the sample, so a read after a write is exact.
- **Only the scans a reading needs.** Waste bins, meters and seismographs skip the nearby-traffic and nearby-people scans, which only traffic, air and noise readings use. A bin's three readings come from one look at it. The readings themselves are unchanged.
- **One snapshot per tick.** The MCP server asks the city for one whole-city snapshot per 100 ms tick, not as many as it can. Per dashboard tick, the city's main thread now spends about 15 ms instead of about 35.
- **Fewer objects to draw.** Simple static device parts are merged per sector-sized chunk with the rest of the scenery, and citizens' work props are not drawn beyond the distance where the whole prop is under about two pixels.
